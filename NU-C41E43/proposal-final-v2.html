The Gap

The business is bigger than the website admits

Clayton Glass has fabricated and installed glass across the Phoenix Valley since 1983: frameless showers, mirrors, windows, patio doors, pool fencing, fire pit glass, countertops and backsplashes. The website shows almost none of it. What's live today is a shower-door brochure built in 2011, and even that doesn't fully work anymore.

What's Happening Now

Today, compared with what we'd build

The site right now
  • –Doesn't display properly on phones, where most people are searching from.
  • –The quote request page doesn't work. There's no way to submit one.
  • –Several menu links open the wrong page entirely: "Gallery" and "FAQ" both lead to the pool fencing page.
  • –Ten near-identical city pages repeat the same paragraph with the city swapped in, and one misspells the city's own name.
  • –It hasn't visibly been touched since 2011. The footer still says so.
  • –There's no way to see which pages visitors look at, or where a call or lead came from.
What we'd build
  • +Fully responsive, built mobile-first, since that's where most visitors already are.
  • +A real quote request flow that reaches you directly, plus click-to-call on every page.
  • +Clean navigation that takes people exactly where they expect.
  • +Two honest service-area pages, Phoenix Metro and Flagstaff / Northern Arizona, written properly.
  • +A modern design that finally looks like a business with four decades behind it.
  • +Built-in tracking, so you can see what's working.

Every visitor who hits a broken quote page or an unreadable mobile menu is a phone call or a quote request that quietly doesn't happen. Right now, there's no way to even know how often that's occurring.

Why This Matters Now

Showing up when someone asks an AI assistant, not just Google

More homeowners are starting the search differently

Instead of scrolling a page of search results, a growing share of people now just ask an assistant directly. Those tools favor sites that are fast, clearly organized by service, and easy to extract a straight answer from. A site built from ten duplicate city pages and a broken contact form is close to invisible to them.

"who installs frameless shower doors in Scottsdale?"

Building to modern standards is what gives Clayton Glass a chance at being the answer to that question, not just a listing on page two. That means real content for each service, clear business information, and a site that loads fast and reads clean.
