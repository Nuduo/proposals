The Gap

The business is bigger than the website admits

Clayton Glass has been fabricating and installing glass across the Phoenix Valley since 1983 — frameless showers, mirrors, windows, patio doors, pool fencing, fire pit glass, countertops and backsplashes. The website shows almost none of that. What's live today is a shower-door brochure built in 2011, and even that doesn't fully work anymore.

What's Actually Happening

Today, compared with what we'd build

The site right now

  • –Doesn't display properly on phones, where most people are actually searching from.
  • –The quote request page doesn't work — there's no way to actually submit one.
  • –Several menu links open the wrong page entirely — "Gallery" and "FAQ" both lead to the pool fencing page.
  • –Ten near-identical city pages repeat the same paragraph with the city swapped in — one misspells the city's own name.
  • –Visibly hasn't been touched since 2011 — the footer still says so.
  • –No way to see which pages visitors look at, or where a call or lead actually came from.

What we'd build

  • +Fully responsive — built mobile-first, since that's where most visitors already are.
  • +A real quote request flow that reaches you directly, plus click-to-call on every page.
  • +Clean navigation that takes people exactly where they expect.
  • +Two honest service-area pages — Phoenix Metro and Flagstaff / Northern Arizona — written properly.
  • +A modern design that reflects four decades in business, not a decade and a half of neglect.
  • +Built-in tracking, so you can see what's actually working.

Every visitor who hits a broken quote page or an unreadable mobile menu is a phone call or a quote request that quietly doesn't happen — and right now, there's no way to even know how often that's occurring.

Why This Matters Now

Showing up when someone asks an AI assistant, not just Google

More homeowners are starting the search differently

Instead of scrolling a page of search results, a growing share of people now just ask an assistant directly. Those tools favor sites that are fast, clearly organized by service, and easy to extract a straight answer from. A site built from ten duplicate city pages and a broken contact form is close to invisible to them.

"who installs frameless shower doors in Scottsdale?"

Building to modern standards — real content per service, clear business information, a site that loads fast and reads clean — is what gives Clayton Glass a chance at being the answer to that question, not just a listing on page two.

How We'd Get There

Six steps, roughly ten to twelve weeks

Discovery

Confirm what's changed since the current site was built — service list, service areas, any photos or reviews already on hand to work with.

Week 1

Design

Map out every page the site needs, then design the look and feel — reviewed with you before anything gets built.

Weeks 2 – 3

Build

Build the actual site — every page, the quote request tool, the photo gallery, the mobile version.

Weeks 4 – 7

Move everything over, safely

Every working link from the old site gets pointed to its new home, so nothing Google already knows about gets lost in the switch.

Week 8

Test

Every page, link, and form checked on real phones and computers before anything goes live.

Week 9

Launch and monitor

Go live, then track calls, quote requests, and search visibility over the following weeks.

Weeks 10 – 12
Next step
