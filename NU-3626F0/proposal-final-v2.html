The Short Version

Good bones, bad plumbing.

We audited all 70 pages on flowheartandvascular.com: every condition, procedure, test, and blog post, along with technical SEO, link health, and content quality. The information architecture is already sound and every page resolves cleanly. What's holding the site back is invisible to a patient and expensive to a practice: structural SEO defects that block search engines from trusting the site, and a content engine that repeats itself so consistently it reads as generated rather than written, even though the underlying medicine is accurate and well-sourced.

What We Found

Five things worth fixing first.

Critical

Search engines can't tell what the practice is

The site's structured data assigns two different entity types to the same identifier on every page, and a third, incomplete version on the About page. That's a broken entity graph, and it directly undermines local-pack ranking and health-related rich results.

Critical

A quarter of the blog isn't credited to the doctor

Six of 22 posts are machine-attributed to a developer account instead of Dr. Patel. The byline reads correctly on the page. The backend record doesn't. For medical content, that's exactly the trust signal search engines and AI assistants check first.

High

The content is already Q&A-shaped and gets no credit for it

Condition and procedure pages already run 9 to 12 question-phrased headers each. None of it carries FAQ schema. This is the single highest-leverage fix available for AI-search visibility: closer to markup than to a rewrite.

High

One template, repeated until it shows

Twenty-plus pages share one skeleton and one rhetorical move. The same "X doesn't mean A, it means B" construction appears on at least seven pages, and FAQ answers open with a bare "Yes." or "No." almost everywhere. The one place Dr. Patel's actual voice comes through is a first-person closing note on a single blog post. It's the best writing on the site, and right now it's the only page that sounds like him.

Medium

The page builder is a performance tax

22 blocking stylesheets and 11 synchronous scripts load on every page. Nothing is broken. It's just weight a rebuild shouldn't inherit.

Also confirmed: title tags, canonicalization, and image alt text are already well executed, and the site auto-generates an llms.txt file for AI-crawler consumption, an advantage almost none of its competitors have. The rebuild keeps what's working.

The Opportunity

Nobody else in this market has done this yet.

We benchmarked eight comparable sites: independent cardiology practices, vein and vascular specialty chains, and two general healthcare UX leaders. None of them have paired real AI-search schema with credentialed physician content. That's genuine white space, and nobody else in this market is even trying to fill it yet.

Credentials Dual board certification, an academic appointment, and 70+ publications outclass every competitor reviewed. Right now the site just lists them. They belong in the first scroll, not buried on the About page.
Second opinion A distinct, lower-commitment service line every competitor treats as one nav item among many. Parsley Health's dual-CTA pattern (two co-equal buttons instead of one generic ask) is the more direct fit here.
Referring physicians Corrielus Cardiology gives referring providers their own primary-nav pathway. Flow Heart has the content already; it just isn't surfaced as its own audience.
Insurance & cost Every vein-clinic competitor names payers or financing by name. Flow Heart's insurance page currently lists plans as plain, unlinked text.
Our Approach

Phase 1 is worth greenlighting on its own regardless of how the larger questions resolve: it's fast, it doesn't foreclose any later platform decision, and it fixes defects that are actively costing search visibility today.
