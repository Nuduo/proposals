Flow Heart & Vascular
Website Rebuild: Audit & Plan

Prepared forFlow Heart & Vascular, Dr. Nachiket J. Patel, MD, FACC, FSCAI
Prepared byNuduo LLC
DateAugust 11, 2026
Job codeNU-3626F0

Based on a full crawl of all 70 sitemap URLs (10 core pages, 22 blog posts, 21 condition pages, 6 diagnostic test pages, 5 procedure pages, 3 taxonomy hubs), a technical SEO audit, a content-quality review, and a competitive scan of eight comparable cardiology/vascular and healthcare sites.

Current site credit line (footer): "Medical Website Design by WebToMed," on WordPress + WP Engine + Cloudflare, running Yoast SEO and an Oxygen/Breakdance page builder. Worth knowing going in: there's an existing vendor relationship, and this plan either replaces or works alongside it.

1The short version

This site is not the mess "rebuild everything" requests usually turn up. The information architecture is already symptom-forward and logically organized. Every one of the 70 sitemap pages resolves cleanly: no 404s, no redirect chains, no orphaned pages. Title tags are disciplined and unique across the board. The site already auto-generates llms.txt for AI-crawler consumption, which almost none of its competitors have. The medical content is factually accurate. Cited trials (RESTOR-1, TORUS 2) and guideline references check out against real sources.

What's actually wrong falls into three buckets, and none of them require throwing out the foundation:

  • Structural SEO defects that are invisible to a human but block machine trust. A JSON-LD @id collision means Google can't cleanly resolve the practice as a single entity. Six of 22 blog posts are structurally attributed to a developer account instead of Dr. Patel. Three pages ship duplicate H1s. Zero pages carry FAQPage schema despite the content already being written in Q&A format almost everywhere.
  • A content engine that repeats itself. Twenty-plus pages were built on one template with one rhetorical toolkit. The same "X doesn't mean A, it means B" construction appears on at least seven pages, and FAQ answers open with a bare "Yes." or "No." almost everywhere. The one place the site lets Dr. Patel actually sound like a physician instead of a byline is a first-person "Bottom Line" close on a single blog post. It's the best writing on the site, and it should be the template rather than the exception.
  • A page-builder tax on performance. Oxygen/Breakdance ships 22 blocking stylesheets and 11 synchronous scripts per page.

2Technical and link health

All 70 URLs return clean 200s with correct canonicalization. Phone links are consistently formatted, NAP is consistent sitewide, and a live Google Business Profile review link confirms an active, claimed listing. Two real issues surfaced: the patient portal link (Healow → eClinicalWorks) lands on a login page carrying leftover "Lively Pediatrics" branding fragments from a shared vendor template, and /book/ is not a scheduling page. It's a landing page for Dr. Patel's upcoming book, "Flow," with an email-signup form. If anyone expects /book/ to mean "book an appointment," that's a naming collision worth fixing regardless of anything else.

4Content quality

Verdict: medium-severity AI-pattern fatigue sitting on top of genuinely accurate clinical material. The two named clinical trials, the publication list, and the guideline citations all check out. The tell is structural, not factual. The same forced-negation move recurs across at least seven pages, the site's central value proposition gets independently regenerated five separate times, and every condition/procedure page follows an identical skeleton closely enough that reading more than three back to back exposes the machinery.

Dr. Patel has 70+ publications, named trial PI roles, and a forthcoming book, and almost none of that shows up in his actual clinical voice. Every condition and procedure page is written in flat third person. The single exception, a first-person "Bottom Line" close on one blog post ("What I want patients to remember is simple..."), is the best-written passage on the entire site. Pulling that voice forward into every page is probably the single highest-value content change available.

5Competitive landscape

Reviewed against three independent/boutique cardiology practices (Corrielus Cardiology, Avicenna/Manhattan Cardiology, Apollo Cardiology), three vein/vascular specialty chains (USA Vein Clinics, Center for Vein Restoration, Metro Vein Centers), and two general healthcare UX benchmarks (Tia, Parsley Health). None of the direct competitors have meaningfully invested in AI-search schema: a real differentiation opportunity, not just catch-up.

  • Dual, co-equal hero CTAs ("Request an Appointment" / "Request a Second Opinion") rather than one generic button, following Parsley's pattern.
  • Credentials as a stat band under the hero, not an About-page bio: the strongest credential combination of any competitor reviewed.
  • A symptom-quiz or self-assessment tool as a low-commitment entry point, standard at every vein-clinic competitor.
  • Explicit insurance/cost language. Every competitor names payers or financing; Flow Heart's insurance page lists plans as plain, unlinked text.
  • A "For Physicians" referral pathway in primary navigation, following Corrielus Cardiology's model.
  • Testimonials with photos and named specificity, closer to Center for Vein Restoration's physician-attributed outcome format.

6Recommended platform

Rebuild on WordPress, but replace the Oxygen/Breakdance page builder with a lean block-based theme. Not headless, not a platform migration. The existing WordPress/Yoast foundation is doing real work already: the llms.txt generation, the clean canonical structure, and the title-tag discipline are assets, not liabilities. The actual defects are fixable within WordPress without a platform change, and a block-based rebuild keeps switching cost low and stays familiar to whichever agency or freelancer maintains content.

If the practice adds associate physicians or wants maximum control over Core Web Vitals and AI-search rendering down the line, a headless WordPress-as-API + Next.js frontend is the natural upgrade path, scoped as Phase 3 rather than the starting point.

7Information architecture

Keep the existing symptom-forward taxonomy. It already tests well against competitors. Changes:

  • Promote Referring Providers to primary nav as "For Physicians," with expanded content on record transfer and consult expectations.
  • Resolve the /book/ naming collision by renaming the book-promotion page and reserve "Book" language exclusively for appointment scheduling.
  • Add a symptom-quiz / self-assessment entry point, separate from the appointment-request form.
  • Rebuild Insurance as a real page with named payers/logos and explicit pricing where comfortable.
  • Add a FAQ hub page plus FAQPage schema wired into every condition, procedure, and diagnostic-test page.
  • Add light local/service-area content for the East Valley (Gilbert, Chandler, Apache Junction, Scottsdale).

8Design direction

The existing photography already has a distinctive palette worth keeping, not replacing. Image alt text describes "warm wood, pale stone, and a deep teal chair" in the consultation room, a stronger, more specific visual identity than the generic blue-and-white medical-site default most competitors use. Lean into that: warm, tactile materials photographed in the real space, a deep teal accent rather than clinical blue, modern typography, and a credentials stat band with icons rather than a dense paragraph. The job is executing the existing palette with a real design system instead of page-builder defaults, not reinventing it.

9Content strategy

Preserve the factual backbone. Fix the delivery:

  • Cut the forced-negation tic and the five-times-restated value proposition; say the core message once and let other pages link to it.
  • Replace the Yes/No-first FAQ format with direct, specific answers.
  • Expand the first-person "Bottom Line" voice from one blog post into a closing section on every condition, procedure, and blog page.
  • Assign each condition page and its overlapping blog post a distinct job to resolve redundancy between them.
  • Bring the Varicose Veins condition page up to the depth of its sibling pages.
  • Reassign the six misattributed blog posts to Dr. Patel's WordPress author account.
